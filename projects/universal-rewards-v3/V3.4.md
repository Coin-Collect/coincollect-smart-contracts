# CoinCollect Universal Rewards V3.4

V3.4 hardens the V3.3 ledger and managed collection contracts. Existing deployments retain their bytecode. The ERC-1155 entitlement split, V2, Treasury, administration, pool accounting, reward assets and 30-day claim deadline keep their original rules. V3.4 introduces a new isolated journal signing domain and requires new contracts when installed.

## Supported collection model

| Chain | ERC-721 | ERC-1155 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Polygon 137 | Standard ERC-165 ERC-721, with a usable live `ownerOf` | Reviewed ERC-1155 with a bound local CoinCollect adapter. Ordinary ERC-1155 is unsupported |
| Base 8453 | Reviewed hooked collection and a registered V3.4 source journal in isolated testing | Reviewed hooked collection and a registered V3.4 source journal in isolated testing |
| Arbitrum One 42161 | Same isolated journal requirements as Base | Same isolated journal requirements as Base |

There is no new mainnet collection certification list. Studio distinguishes a declared standard, a verified registry binding and an available isolated journal. ERC-165 alone does not establish that a custom implementation reports every mutation. Review the collection's complete path for mint, burn, transfer, batch transfer and duplicate IDs. The local hook must run **before receiver callbacks** and propagate settlement failures. Its binding must be immutable once enrolled. Audit a proxy implementation and its upgrade authority separately. The adapter pins local runtime bytecode. A proxy can change behavior without changing its runtime hash. The ledger caps its ownership lookup at 50,000 gas. Verify the hook and balance reads within that budget.

For ordinary ERC-1155, `balanceOf` cannot reconstruct which participating units left across transfers, burns and subsequent top-ups. ERC-1155 receiver hooks notify the receiver. An external reward adapter has no guaranteed execution path whenever an ordinary token changes hands. Event replay is a possible non-custodial route if a future protocol can prove finalized receipts, complete ordered log coverage, and a source-authorized action against that ordered state. An indexer assertion, snapshots alone, or an incomplete log proof leave those requirements open. This release adds no custody, NFT approvals, or asynchronous fallback that discards rights. [ERC-1155 standard](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1155) · [Canonical block reads](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1898)

## Transfer safety

- Expired pools are skipped before settlement. The live adapter reuses a tracked pool's immutable schedule and collection power, avoiding repeated full pool reads. There are at most eight tracked pools per collection through the end of their claim windows. A ninth slot is rejected on enrollment and becomes available when a previous claim window expires.
- A ledger error, an out-of-gas transfer, or an ERC-1155 receiver rejection rolls back both NFT and reward state. A receiver with custody during its callback can make a real onward transfer. The forwarded units retain their rights, and an impersonated adapter callback is rejected.
- Pausing Treasury does not interrupt local NFT transfers. A hook can still make a valid token transfer revert if settlement fails or a sufficiently dense batch exceeds the gas limit. This synchronous dependency is a compatibility requirement. Estimate and split large batches of enrolled IDs. Preserve the atomic hook and never silently swallow a revert.
- Once the claim deadline is reached, the transfer no longer touches the expired pool's claims. A same-block race at the deadline rejects claim and permits permissionless Treasury recovery. An ERC-1155 transfer on the source journal can exceed uint128. Its recorded amount saturates at uint128 max, which still covers the sender's entire tracked entitlement.

## Cross-chain settlement

Witnesses sign the source block, canonical hash, timestamp, finalized sequence, contiguous entries, collection, adapter and journal domain. The Polygon verifier rejects altered first indices, previous heads, conflicting facts for the same height, missing signatures and duplicate signers. The witness helper independently checks finalized predecessor continuity and the canonical log order. A full batch can legitimately leave an unapplied suffix. No released claim is payable until that known suffix is consumed. A paused or two-hour-old finalized head closes claims. A fresh signed checkpoint can reopen them without a user event. A signed checkpoint with no entries cannot advance the cursor.

A known eligibility failure or an expired intent can be skipped with an on-chain event. Unknown failures and out-of-gas retain the whole batch for retry. Transfer observations always fail atomically. A quorum can still attest false facts at a new height. On-chain code does not independently verify rollup finality. Witness key segregation, independent nodes, incident response and an independent audit are still required. During an outage, halt the affected stream and check both RPC providers against the last accepted block before attempting recovery. Production remote execution remains prohibited. [Base finality](https://docs.base.org/specifications/transactions/transaction-finality) · [Arbitrum finality](https://docs.arbitrum.io/build-decentralized-apps/troubleshooting-building)

Recorded power and distinct-wallet thresholds change when the accepted event is applied on Polygon. A later proof does not rewrite past indexes. In the actual source-chain fixture, a 600-second delay kept recorded participation at one wallet against a threshold of two. Aggregate power was 53. The pool accrued **30,000,000** base units across that interval, compared with **60,000,000** if both wallets had been observed throughout. Accrued liabilities were therefore lower by 30,000,000 base units. Once all transfers were applied, the recorded wallets became two, with power split 37 to 16. If a cap also changes the active power, the same lag affects each position's share through its recorded power divided by total recorded power. The two-hour payout gate bounds the age of admitted evidence. Transfer lag and distortion in past emissions have no fixed upper bound during a source outage.

## Measured gas and illustrative costs

Measurements are from receipts. Base and Arbitrum use separate Anvil chains with real source transactions and common finalized block reads. Their RPC aliases share the same fixture node. Production L1 fees and independent operator costs are outside these measurements. The scaling benchmark uses generated journal records. The Polygon price-path benchmark uses a read-only upstream Polygon fork at block 97,638,790 and writes only to that local fork.

Dollar examples are scenarios, assuming one USDT is one dollar. The quiet Polygon scenario uses 30 gwei and $0.25/POL. The stressed scenario uses 200 gwei and $0.50/POL. An illustrative Base/Arbitrum execution rate of 0.01 gwei and $3,000/ETH gives about $0.00218 for join plus release. Add rollup L1 and applicable operator fees using real transaction quotes. [Base fee formula](https://docs.base.org/specifications/transactions/network-fees) · [Arbitrum execution and L1 fees](https://docs.arbitrum.io/build-decentralized-apps/troubleshooting-building)

- **$10 pool with 4 equal positions.** Gross maximum is $2.50 per position. A source request pair plus an unshared Polygon lifecycle of roughly 1.46M gas costs about $0.013 quiet or $0.149 stressed before L1 fees. The user and relay operator can bear different parts of that total.
- **$10 pool with 1,024 equal positions.** Gross maximum is $0.00977 per position. In an illustrative bulk scenario, amortizing eight joins and adding one release and claim gives about 866,000 Polygon gas per position. Including the two source executions, that is about $0.00867 quiet or $0.08878 stressed before L1 fees. The quiet margin is roughly $0.0011. For a $1,000 pool at the same size, the gross ceiling rises to $0.9766 per position.
- **$10 Treasury Auto-Swap over 365 days.** At three purchases per 30-day interval, projecting 13 intervals from the measured tranche gives about 21.35M purchase gas, or $0.16 quiet and $2.13 stressed. Adding all eight observed TWAP transactions to each interval would give about 30.31M gas, or $0.23 and $3.03. Oracle observations may be shared, and small final trades can fail route or fee checks. The worker already caps a Treasury purchase fee at the lower of 0.25 POL and 5% of protected input value. At stressed prices, the measured lanes would need roughly $0.77–$1.27 of input per trade even assuming a protected quote at face value. The actual first inputs were 0.246576 WPOL lane, 0.410959 COLLECT lane and 0.164384 SHIB lane, all denominated in USDT. The stressed case defers these purchases under the fee guard.
- **Idle remote proof refresh.** At about 70k gas per proof and an optimistic 12 refreshes per day, keeping one stream continuously available would require about 306.8M Polygon gas per year before relay work, or $2.30 quiet and $30.68 stressed. On-demand refresh can reduce operating spend with temporary claim delays. Witness infrastructure is additional.
